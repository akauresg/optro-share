Vendor status — Due Diligence taxonomy

Six statuses. Color encodes who has the next move (ball in court). The badge text names the lifecycle position. The activity line under the badge tells you what is happening right now. Status transitions split cleanly into natural actions (Engage, Accept tier, Approve, Reject, Offboard) and overrides (rare, audit-friction-bearing).

The six vendor statuses

Pre-engagement

Requested

Vendor was created (Zip, manual, or migration). Awaiting triage decision (Engage or Decline). Ball is on you.

Active work

Due Diligence

Engaged. Going through risk classification, evidence gathering, response drafting, vendor gap-fill, review, and findings. Badge color shifts with ball-in-court inside this state.

Live

Approved

Verdict reached, vendor live and continuously monitored. Reassessment cycles run inside this state without flipping the status.

Terminal · pre-due-diligence

Declined

Closed at intake without ever entering due diligence. Record persists for audit and funnel reporting.

Terminal · post-review

Rejected

Closed after due diligence, due to a failed verdict or unresolvable blocking findings. Re-openable as a remediation path.

Terminal · post-approval

Offboarded

Was Approved, is no longer in use. Reached via manual action with a reason captured. Becomes the output of an offboarding workflow when those capabilities exist.

Inside Due Diligence — what the activity line shows

Due Diligence is the longest-running state and the one where the agent actively does work. Risk classification (was previously called Inherent Risk) is the first thing that happens inside it, not its own status. The status name stays constant; the badge color and the activity line carry the moment-to-moment reality.

Why this works. Risk classification, evidence gathering, drafting, vendor wait, review, and findings are the same continuous arc of evaluating a vendor. Customers and auditors call this "due diligence." The activity line carries which step we're on; the status carries the lifecycle position. Adding new sub-steps later (e.g., legal review, business owner signoff) does not expand the taxonomy; it adds a new activity-line variant.

The pattern. The primary button on each profile is the action a TPRM analyst would take 90% of the time. Engage moves Requested into Due Diligence and kicks off the AI workflow. Accept Tier commits the AI's risk classification. Approve and Reject are the verdict. Offboard (in the kebab on Approved) is the natural way to close a live vendor. These are not overrides. They are the workflow.

Natural action vs override — two different patterns

Most status changes are natural. A small set are overrides. The interaction patterns are deliberately different so the user can feel the friction difference.

The friction is the signal. The natural Engage modal is short, blue-primary, no warning. The override modal is taller, amber-primary, with explicit audit notice. A user knows by the shape of the modal which kind of action they are taking. The system meets them where the action is, instead of forcing every status change through one undifferentiated dialog.

After an override — where the manual indicator lives

The override fact lives in three places: the small meta line at the top of the profile, the activity-line description (which carries the actual situation including who set it and why), and the timeline (full audit). It does not sit inline next to the status badge. The status reads cleanly as Approved.

First-principles summary

Six statuses, stable

Requested, Due Diligence, Approved, Declined, Rejected, Offboarded. No intake or inherent risk as separate statuses; those are sub-steps inside Due Diligence carried by the activity line.

Color = ball-in-court

Blue for you, purple for the AI, yellow for the vendor, orange for the reviewer, green for live, muted for closed. The list view's color column tells you what to focus on without reading any text.

Activity line carries detail

"AI drafting responses — 142 of 200." "Awaiting vendor — 14 questions out, reminder in 2 days." "Resolving findings — 3 blocking open." Driven by sub-objects, not stored on the vendor.

Two interaction patterns

Natural transitions are primary CTAs in the header: Engage, Accept tier, Approve, Reject, Offboard. Overrides are a tucked-away menu, friction-bearing, audit-logged. The shape of the modal tells you which kind of action you are taking.
